What Should I Eat Before and After a Workout?

Think of your body like a high-performance sports car. Before driving, you need quick fuel so you don't stall out. After a long drive, you need fuel to repair the engine and refill the tank.

💡 The Action Plan

  • 30–60 minutes before: eat fast-digesting carbs with a small touch of protein for immediate energy.Try this: a banana with a spoonful of peanut butter, or oatmeal topped with berries.
  • 30–60 minutes after: eat a balanced meal rich in protein (to repair muscle tears) and carbs (to restock stored energy).Try this: chicken with rice and veggies, eggs on toast, or a protein shake blended with fruit.

Ref — International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN)

02/11

How Do I Lose Belly Fat?

Here's the honest truth: you cannot "spot-reduce" fat from just your stomach. Your body burns fat like pulling water out of a swimming pool — the water level drops evenly everywhere, not just in one corner. Doing 1,000 sit-ups strengthens your abs, but it won't melt the fat layer sitting on top of them.

💡 The Action Plan

Focus on lowering your overall body fat percentage. Stay in a consistent, mild calorie deficit — eating slightly fewer calories than your body burns each day. Pair healthy nutrition with 7–8 hours of sleep, plenty of water, and strength training to see total-body results.

Ref — Harvard Health Publishing

03/11

How Do I Build an Exercise Plan?

The secret to any successful workout program is progressive overload — a fancy term for making your workouts slightly harder over time so your body is forced to grow stronger.

💡 The Action Plan

  1. Pick a schedule: choose 3–4 days a week that you can stick to consistently.
  2. Focus on big movements: build each session around 4–5 core exercises that hit big muscle groups — pushing, pulling, squatting, hinging.
  3. Set sets and reps: aim for 3 sets of 8–12 repetitions per exercise.
  4. Progress weekly: when 12 reps start feeling too easy, add a little extra weight or try for one extra rep next time.

Ref — National Academy of Sports Medicine (NASM)

04/11

How Much Should I Work Out a Week?

Working out 7 days a week actually slows down your progress. Muscles don't grow while you're lifting weights — they grow while you're resting, eating, and sleeping.

💡 The Action Plan

Aim for 3 to 5 days a week for about 45–60 minutes per session. Always schedule at least 2 full rest or light recovery days — like walking or light stretching — each week to avoid injury and burnout.

Ref — American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM)

05/11

What Exercises Are Good for Building Muscle?

You want to focus on compound exercises — movements that force multiple joints and muscles to work at the same time. These give you the most muscle activation in the shortest amount of time.

💡 The Action Plan

  • Legs: squats, Romanian deadlifts, lunges.
  • Chest & triceps: bench press, push-ups, dips.
  • Back & biceps: pull-ups, lat pulldowns, dumbbell rows.
  • Shoulders: overhead presses, lateral raises.

Ref — Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research

06/11

Which Exercise Burns the Most Fat?

High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) and running burn the most calories per minute. However, the best exercise for long-term fat loss is whatever activity you enjoy enough to do consistently over months.

💡 The Action Plan

Combine strength training 3 days a week (to keep your muscle mass) with daily active movement. Aim for 8,000–10,000 steps a day. Walking is one of the ultimate fat-loss tools because it burns calories without leaving you exhausted or ravenous.

Ref — Mayo Clinic

07/11

How Much Protein Do I Need?

When you exercise, you create tiny, microscopic tears in your muscle fibers. Protein provides the essential building blocks (amino acids) your body uses to repair those tears so you come back stronger and firmer.

💡 The Action Plan

Target roughly 0.7–1.0 gram of protein per pound of body weight each day. If you weigh 150 lbs, aim for about 105–150 grams of protein daily, spread across meals — chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, beans, or protein shakes.

Ref — Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition

08/11

Can I Lose Weight Without Exercising?

Yes. Weight loss comes down to energy balance. If you eat fewer calories than your body burns living daily life, you will lose weight, even if you never step foot in a gym.

💡 The Action Plan

While diet controls weight loss, adding resistance training ensures you lose fat instead of muscle. Keeping your muscle keeps your metabolism high and gives you a strong, toned physique.

Ref — National Institutes of Health (NIH)

09/11

How Do I Get a Six-Pack?

Everyone already has abdominal muscles. Visible abs are simply a matter of getting your overall body fat low enough for those muscles to show through your skin.

💡 The Action Plan

Treat your abs like any other muscle: train them 2–3 times a week with exercises like hanging leg raises, cable crunches, or planks. Pair that training with a sensible diet to lean down over time.

Ref — American Council on Exercise (ACE)

10/11

What Is the Best Way to Lose Weight?

Avoid extreme crash diets that cut out entire food groups or starve you. Rapid weight loss usually leads to fast weight regain — slow and steady progress wins every time.

💡 The Action Plan

  • Eat in a modest calorie deficit (roughly 300–500 calories below maintenance).
  • Prioritize high protein and high fiber — veggies, fruits, whole grains — to stay full.
  • Lift weights 3 times a week to keep your muscle.
  • Get 7–9 hours of quality sleep to control stress and hunger hormones.

Ref — Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)

11/11

What Are the Best Workouts to Do at Home?

You don't need expensive machines or a heavy gym membership to get in shape. Using your own body weight through a full range of motion builds solid, functional strength.

💡 The Action Plan

Try this simple 4-round bodyweight circuit at home:

  1. 10–15 push-ups (on toes or knees).
  2. 15–20 bodyweight squats.
  3. 10 chair dips (for triceps).
  4. 10 inverted rows (using a sturdy table or door frame).
  5. 45-second plank.

Ref — Journal of Sports Science & Medicine
